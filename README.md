One word for the human you work for. The program writes it to `/output/entry.txt`:

> Quickquestioner

Every boss, client and manager opens with "got a quick question?" — and the question is never quick. The joke is inside the word itself, and it names a scene every worker recognises instantly.

## Method

Twelve original coined words were drafted against the judge's brief, each hand-scored 0–10 on the brief's five criteria in order (one word on the subject / funny / fits / vivid / lands without explanation):

| Word | Subject | Funny | Fits | Vivid | Lands | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Quickquestioner** | 10 | 9 | 10 | 9 | 9 | **47** |
| Weekendtexter | 10 | 8 | 9 | 9 | 10 | 46 |
| Circlebacker | 10 | 9 | 8 | 9 | 9 | 45 |
| Scopecreeper | 10 | 8 | 8 | 9 | 8 | 43 |
| Briefchanger | 10 | 7 | 9 | 8 | 9 | 43 |
| Ideahaver | 10 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 42 |
| Checkinner | 10 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 42 |
| Revisioner | 10 | 7 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 41 |
| Paychecksigner | 10 | 6 | 9 | 7 | 9 | 41 |
| Urgentsender | 10 | 7 | 8 | 7 | 8 | 40 |
| Fridayfiver | 10 | 8 | 7 | 8 | 6 | 39 |
| Deadlinemover | 10 | 6 | 8 | 7 | 7 | 38 |

**Revision pass:** the first draft worried "questioner" alone could read as "one who questions quickly". The word was kept: in a work context the fixed phrase "quick question" dominates the reading, and the shorter alternative "Quickasker" loses the joke because "quick asker" is not a phrase anyone says. The runner-up, Weekendtexter, leans boss-heavy, while Quickquestioner covers the whole brief — boss, client, manager, whoever signs off.
